On the evening of February 18, 1989, I phoned my future second wife and asked: I will dictate, and you will sketch. A point, many points, a line, many lines, a plane.
Is that all? There are only five lines here.
How many are needed?
Seven or eight. Eight is better.
I will think about it.

That is how the great theory of levels of abstract intelligence (TLAI) was born — also known as the ladder of complexity, also known as the levels of the field of attention

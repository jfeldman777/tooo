This understanding of the human psyche makes it easier to interpret classic results.
Here is a good example from Jean Piaget. Before a child, the psychologist pours water from a wide glass into a narrow one — the water level rises. Asked "Is there more?", 5-year-olds say "More!", and 8-year-olds say "not more, the same." Piaget explains: "at five children do not yet know the laws of nature, in particular conservation of matter, and at eight they already do." My comment:
the human brain is not built to understand laws of nature in such an abstract form. It is not about that at all. So where does the difference come from?

At 5 the child sees objects but not the process; at 8 they see the process. If water lives its own life, then in that process water remains water — there is still the same amount.
If we see the wide glass and the narrow glass as two separate objects, then comparing height in one and height in the other we conclude "there is more." It is not about physics or logic. It is about different fields of attention.

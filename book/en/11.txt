Now I will try to show that arithmetic relates to algebra as one process relates to a family of processes.
Here is an arithmetic problem with a solution.
There was an unknown number (of apples); five more were brought, and there were ten. How many apples were there at the start.
Arithmetic solution. We have two addends and a sum. To find the unknown addend, subtract the known addend from the sum.
From 10 subtract 5 and get 5 (apples)
Algebraic solution.
There were x apples; five more were brought — there were 10.
We write the equation
x + 5 = 10

What is the meaning of this equation?
We go through the natural numbers (1, 2, ...)
and substitute into the left side.
1+5 = 6
2+5 = 7
3+5 = 8
4+5 = 9
5+5 = 10 Stop!
The original amount is 5.
That is, behind one equation stands a family of numerical statements, almost all of them false, only one true — and you must find it.
But if you do not picture the family of relations — the whole procedure loses its meaning.

Natural science (including mathematics) and the humanities (including philosophy) — in real history — differ like heaven and earth.
From a neutral observer's point of view, this at least requires explanation.
While we stay within psychology, we cannot answer this question. But we can pull a trick — move from psychology into the history of natural science — and everything becomes clear.
At the origins of physics there are examples that shed light on the problem. For example, the story of Kepler and Newton. Kepler analyzed numbers and found formulas for them. Newton built a model — and within that model mathematically and logically derived Kepler's formulas. Kepler did half the work — Newton the second half.
Humanists are convinced that Newton discovered a law of nature, but that is a mistake. Newton discovered a model; a thought experiment on that model led him to Kepler's formulas.

Of course, the data mass Kepler and Newton relied on far exceeds the data volume a single psychologist rests on. But there is a solution to this problem. We need to gather data from different psychologists. But because each psychologist describes data in their own special language, the data cannot be combined. A way out? Translating dialects into a single language removes this limit. What prevents creating a single language?
If you are a member of a community, they will not let you — for a community language is the highest value — but for a person outside the community (me) nothing stands in the way, as we will see later.
